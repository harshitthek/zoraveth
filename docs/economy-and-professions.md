# Economy and Professions Specification

## Economic identity
Use a hybrid economy: players can gather, craft, trade and specialize, while design safeguards prevent monopolies over essential content and first-discovery rewards.

## Gathering and resources
- Common resources should be reliably obtainable through personal or shared gathering.
- Rare resources may be shared, contested or event-driven.
- Avoid permanent resource monopolies.
- Resource availability can react to ecosystem conditions, time, weather and player activity within readable limits.
- Gathering should give feedback and have a clear place in exploration.

## Crafting
- Basic crafting is accessible.
- Specialist progression supports deep mastery.
- Rare recipes/materials and masterwork gear reward research and commitment.
- Any class can learn professions.
- Basics may be taught publicly; advanced specialties can be found through research, experimentation, relationships or discovery.
- Structured recipes coexist with discoveries from compatible materials, tools and environments.
- Recipe sharing should be player-controlled and knowledge may spread naturally.
- Apprenticeship and mentorship can create lasting traditions.
- Player-created schools may gain social recognition without requiring a complex dedicated institution system.

## Player-driven market
- Ordinary resources, crafted equipment and eligible tradeable loot can be traded.
- Exclusive first-discovery credit and selected legendary rewards may be non-tradeable or restricted.
- Avoid pay-to-win mechanics and selling rare discoveries for real money.
- Monitor inflation, botting, resource concentration and manipulation if an economy is implemented at scale.

## Knowledge economy
Players may trade maps, guides, research and discovered recipes. However:
- Core progression remains independently attainable.
- Optional knowledge can save time or reveal alternate routes without making other players permanently dependent.
- The game should support consent-based sharing and organic rumours.

## Prototype scope
Do not build a player market, currency balancing or a profession tree during the first prototype. Begin with one gatherable resource and simple feedback; add inventory only when gameplay requires it.
