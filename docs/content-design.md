# World and Content Design

## World structure
- Layered geography can include a continent, islands, distant lands, underground civilizations, deep networks and hidden regions.
- Shallow caves/mines are accessible earlier; deeper layers may contain inhabited civilizations and ancient horrors.
- Multiple peoples/civilizations interact; avoid reducing every culture to a single stereotype.
- Exact geography and names remain open.

## Ancestries and character creation
- Long-term goal: approximately 5–7 playable ancestries at launch, with balanced traits and trade-offs.
- Character creation should offer quick presets and optional deeper customization.
- Appearance is flexible; ancestry traits should not create one universally optimal choice.
- Earned transformations may exist later, without making basic identity customization punitive.

## Classes
- Choose a core class at character creation.
- Class switching may eventually be possible through trainers/quests/specialized services with costs; earlier mastery remains valuable.
- Long-term target around 8–10 core classes with branching paths and possibly secret paths.
- First prototype only implements Spellblade.

## Regions and exploration
- Settlements and roads are mapped; wilderness, ruins and dungeons are progressively discovered.
- Travel begins with physical exploration; mounts, shortcuts, waypoints and fast travel may become available after places are established.
- Mounts/companions can aid traversal/support; strongest forms should be earned.
- Safe regions should feel warm and lively; wilderness should feel beautiful, uncertain and dangerous.
- Hybrid map: known settled areas mapped, unexplored places discovered through travel and clues.

## Quests and choices
- Ordinary quests can have clear objectives and optional markers.
- Side stories and hidden quests can emerge organically from NPCs, objects and environmental clues.
- Choices may affect personal relationships and selected regional/shared outcomes.
- Preserve access to important content even when the world changes.
- Player identity/customization exists within an authored mystery, not instead of it.

## Stealth and environmental interaction
- Long-term stealth can include scouting, infiltration, traps, disguises, lockpicking, pickpocketing and alternate quest routes.
- Basic stealth can be universal while specialized classes excel.
- Environmental systems may include fire, noise, terrain and traps.
- Elemental rules should be consistent (e.g., water affecting lightning, ice changing movement, wind redirecting selected effects, fire changing vegetation) and have readable counterplay.

## Discoveries
- Common hidden quests and extraordinary cryptic secrets coexist.
- Environmental clues and player experimentation should support discovery.
- Optional hints may help without revealing the complete solution.
- Track first clues/triggers/contributions where useful.
- Personal discoveries coexist with permanent shared-world milestones and repeatable/accessibility alternatives.
- Legendary encounters may be found through legends, ecology, ruins, conditions, NPCs and accidental triggers; triggers should be deliberate or accidental but fair and explainable.

## NPCs and factions
- NPCs provide useful services but have routines and motivations beyond the player.
- Reputation can vary by region/faction.
- Factions can be flexible, with hidden societies.
- NPCs may hold conflicting or incomplete beliefs.
- The elderly stranger is an exception only in narrative importance, not proof that all NPCs are secretive.
